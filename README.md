### 🏨 Hajzak (حجزك) | Advanced multi-platform hospitality and reservations ecosystem

**An integrated hospitality software ecosystem—not merely a hotel-booking website.** Hajzak was conceived around the complete journey from property discovery to reservation handling and the operational tools required by accommodation providers.

The project included interconnected **Android and iOS mobile experiences for customers and hotel/property operators**, alongside **management and administration interfaces** for coordinating accommodation listings, availability-related information, booking processes, and the day-to-day operation of hotels and chalets.

**Key system areas**
- **Customer-facing mobile applications:** hotel and chalet exploration, property information, search and reservation-oriented user journeys.
- **Hotel and property-side applications:** tools for participating accommodation providers to manage and respond to relevant operational and booking workflows.
- **Administrative and control interfaces:** a separate management layer connecting users, listed properties, and the reservation platform.
- **Advanced discovery and data access:** a more sophisticated **indexing/search design**, with **Redis** used within the system's technology stack, aimed at responsive lookup and scalable data-access patterns.
- **Multi-application integration:** coordinating distinct user roles and mobile platforms around shared business processes and backend services.

**Engineering focus:** cross-platform product architecture · hospitality domain modeling · advanced indexing · Redis integration · booking workflows · multi-role applications · management interfaces.

*Selected historical work. No public source release is implied, and this overview does not assert independently benchmarked search performance or current commercial availability.*

### 🛍️ BASAT (بساط) | Dynamic, multi-vendor marketplace and application-management platform

**A complete commerce-oriented software ecosystem designed around dynamic application composition—not a fixed storefront with a few configurable banners.**

BASAT brings together a **C#/.NET backend**, administrative and merchant-facing capabilities, a **Flutter customer application**, and a **separate Flutter courier application**. It encompasses business workflows around merchant/store management, product catalogs, customer orders, delivery, reporting, and other marketplace operations.

A defining part of the project is its **data-driven storefront and application management model**:

- **Dynamic page composition:** storefront pages and their presentation can be structured and managed through configuration instead of requiring every layout change to be hardcoded into the mobile app.
- **Flexible home-page sections:** independently configurable page sections, their ordering, content placement, and display arrangements.
- **Dynamic promotions and offers:** management of merchandising content, promotional areas, and changing offers within the customer experience.
- **Centralized management:** administrative workflows that coordinate the customer-facing experience with merchant operations, order handling, and reporting.
- **Integrated applications:** a customer-facing commerce experience and a separate delivery/courier workflow connected to the backend.
- **Commerce domain depth:** supporting workflows described in earlier project records include payments, shipping, reviews, messaging, and accounting-oriented operations.

The **product experience and marketplace design were inspired by the breadth and presentation patterns of noon and similar large regional commerce applications**. This does **not** imply affiliation with noon or reuse of its proprietary software.

**Engineering focus:** ASP.NET / C# backend · API-driven Flutter apps · configurable UI/page architecture · dynamic sections and merchandising · multi-vendor commerce · courier integration · operational reporting.

*Private source. Selected BASAT modules and application structure are inspectable in my private repositories; these project descriptions are not claims that every historical capability was independently validated in production.*
